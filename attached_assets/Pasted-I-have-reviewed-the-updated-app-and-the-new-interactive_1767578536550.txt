### Advanced Solver Behavior & Diagnostics

#### 1. **Convergence Iteration "Snapshots" for ICM**
*   **Purpose:** To show how ICM's Newton-Raphson iterative solver converges on an answer for a single timestep, contrasting with SWMM5's successive relaxation.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A simple node with two connecting pipes. A known inflow is applied.
    *   The state (Head`H`, Flows`Q1, Q2`) is initially`NaN`.
    *   **Button:** **"Solve This Timestep"**.
    *   **Animation:** For each iteration`i`:
        1.  The calculated heads and flows are displayed with a "guess" value.
        2.  A "Residual" value (a measure of error) is shown graphically as a shrinking bar.
        3.  The values snap closer to the final solution.
    *   **Side Note:** A static callout for SWMM5 could explain it uses "successive relaxation with under-relaxation factors" instead of a full Newton method.
*   **Educational Goal:** Demystifies "The simulation engine is iterating..." and illustrates why ICM is often more stable for ill-conditioned networks.

#### 2. **Mass Balance Error Tracker & Source Explorer**
*   **Purpose:** To visualize the elusive concept of mass balance error, where it comes from (truncation, tolerances), and how solver choices affect it.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A single storage node or a small 3-pipe network.
    *   A **water balance bar chart** animates over a simulated storm:
        *   **Cumulative Inflow** (blue bar grows)
        *   **Cumulative Outflow** (green bar grows)
        *   **System Storage** (yellow bar fluctuates)
        *   **Mass Error** (red bar, small):`(Initial Storage + Inflow) - (Final Storage + Outflow)`
    *   **Sliders/Controls:**
        *`Numerical Tolerance` (affects error size).
        *`Routing Step` in SWMM5 mode /`Minimum Timestep` in ICM mode.
        *`Dry Depth` (tiny flows that get "lost").
    *   **Interaction:** As users make the simulation "rougher" (larger timestep, looser tolerance), the red **Mass Error** bar visibly grows.
*   **Educational Goal:** Makes a critical model performance metric visible and understandable.

### Comparative Scenario Simulators

#### 3. **"Oscillation Challenge" for Hydraulic Jump/Surcharge**
*   **Purpose:** To create a classic test case where SWMM5's node-link method can oscillate, and show how ICM's distributed solver damps it.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A **vertical pipe** (water column) or a **rapidly filling storage tank**.
    *   A powerful, abrupt inflow is introduced.
    *   **Two synchronized graphs** show the water surface elevation over time:
        *   **Graph A (SWMM5 Logic):** Shows potential for strong, decaying oscillation as the water "sloshes" due to the concentrated node inertia.
        *   **Graph B (ICM Logic):** Shows a heavily damped, monotonic rise due to internal distributed friction and the Preissmann slot.
    *   **Controls:** Inflow pulse magnitude and duration.
*   **Educational Goal:** Demonstrates a practical stability difference in a recognizable scenario.

#### 4. **Dry Startup / Wetting Front Propagation**
*   **Purpose:** To compare how each solver handles the initiation of flow in a dry, flat conduit—a common pain point.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A long, flat pipe, initially colored gray (dry).
    *   A small, constant inflow is introduced at the upstream end.
    *   **Two side-by-side animations:**
        *   **SWMM5:** The wetting front moves in a blocky, stepwise fashion. The downstream end remains dry (`Q=0`) until the front conceptually arrives at the node.
        *   **ICM:** The wetting front propagates more smoothly as a shallow film (`Base Flow`) down the entire pipe length, with depth gradually increasing.
    *   **Control:** Pipe slope and roughness.
*   **Educational Goal:** Shows why ICM often starts more easily in dry systems and the conceptual difference in handling initial conditions.
