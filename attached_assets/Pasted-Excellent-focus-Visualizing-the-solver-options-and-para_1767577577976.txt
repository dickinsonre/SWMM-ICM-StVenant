### SWMM5 Solution Options & Controls

These diagrams would explain the knobs you turn in the`[OPTIONS]` section of an SWMM5 INP file or its GUI dialog.

#### 1. **Routing Step & Stability "CFL Condition" Visualizer**
*   **Purpose:** To illustrate the critical relationship between time step (`Route Step`), flow wave speed, and model stability.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A simple pipe is shown with a flow wave moving through it.
    *   **Sliders for:**
        *`Route Step` (e.g., 1 to 60 seconds)
        *   Pipe Length (`L`)
        *   Wave Speed (`c`, influenced by depth/diameter)
    *   **Visual Output:** The diagram calculates and displays the **Courant Number** (`Cr = c * Δt / L`).
    *   **Interaction:** As the user adjusts sliders, a gauge or color code indicates:
        *   **Green (Stable):**`Cr <= 1`
        *   **Yellow (Risky):**`1 < Cr < 1.5`
        *   **Red (Unstable - Model Crashes):**`Cr >> 1`
    *   **Educational Goal:** Teaches why SWMM5 often requires short, fixed time steps and what "CFL condition violation" means.

#### 2. **Surcharge Method Selector & Impact**
*   **Purpose:** To compare the **"Preissmann Slot"** option vs. the traditional **"Surcharge Algorithm"**.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A single manhole (node) and a connecting conduit.
    *   A toggle switch: **"Use Preissmann Slot"** (Yes/No).
    *   A slider controls the water level rising above the pipe crown (surcharge).
    *   **When "NO" (Traditional):**
        *   Show water level hitting the node's "surcharge depth." A note appears: "Continuity solved using dQ/dH derivative. No physical slot."
        *   The head in the node rises rapidly, but the pipe cross-section visual does not change.
    *   **When "YES" (Preissmann Slot):**
        *   Show the virtual slot appearing as water passes the crown.
        *   The head rises more smoothly, with a note: "Pressurized wave speed`c = sqrt(g*A/T)` maintained for stability."
    *   **Educational Goal:** Clarifies a major advanced option and its effect on numerical behavior during pressurization.

#### 3. **Routing Method Selection Flowchart**
*   **Purpose:** To guide users on when to choose **Dynamic Wave**, **Kinematic Wave**, or **Steady Flow**.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A decision-tree flowchart with questions:
        1.  **"Does backwater, flow reversal, or pressure flow matter?"** (Yes → Dynamic Wave)
        2.  **"Are slopes steep (>0.1%) and system primarily gutter/overland?"** (Yes → Kinematic Wave)
        3.  **"Is it a simple, steady-state calculation?"** (Yes → Steady Flow)
    *   **Interaction:** Clicking answers highlights the path and reveals a **typical use-case image** (e.g., for Dynamic Wave, show a surcharged pipe system; for Kinematic, show overland sheet flow).
    *   **Educational Goal:** Transforms a menu option into an engineering decision aid.

### InfoWorks ICM Simulation Parameters

These diagrams would explain the parameters found in the ICM UI under`Model Parameters | Simulation Parameters` or the 2D parameters dialog.

#### 1. **Adaptive Timestep "Governor" Diagram**
*   **Purpose:** To demystify how ICM's adaptive time-stepping works.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A timeline of a simulation with a hydrograph showing a sharp peak.
    *   **Key Parameters as Sliders:**`Minimum Timestep`,`Maximum Timestep`,`Convergence Tolerance`.
    *   **Visualization:** The timeline shows the **actual timestep length** as a bar height above it.
        *   During dry/steady flow: The bar is tall (timestep at`Maximum`).
        *   As the wave enters: Bars shrink rapidly to`Minimum`.
        *   As convergence is achieved: Bars grow again.
    *   **Interaction:** Adjusting`Convergence Tolerance` tighter makes the bars shrink sooner and for longer. Increasing`Max Timestep` allows longer steps in quiet periods.
    *   **Educational Goal:** Shows how ICM balances speed and accuracy dynamically, contrasting with SWMM5's fixed step.

#### 2. **The "Theta (θ)" Parameter in the Preissmann Scheme**
*   **Purpose:** To show the effect of the implicit/explicit weighting factor on numerical damping and stability.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A simple wave (a sharp peak) moves along a 1D channel.
    *   **Slider:**`Theta (θ)` from 0.5 (Crank-Nicolson, less damping) to 1.0 (Fully Implicit, more damping).
    *   **Two lines animate over the wave:** One shows the "true" theoretical wave, the other shows the numerically simulated wave.
        *   At`θ = 0.6` (ICM's typical ~0.65): The simulated wave slightly lags and smooths the peak.
        *   At`θ = 0.5`: Less smoothing, but the wave may show small oscillations (numerical dispersion).
        *   At`θ = 1.0`: The wave is heavily damped, significantly reducing the peak.
    *   **Educational Goal:** Explains a core numerical parameter most users never touch but governs solution "sharpness."

#### 3. **1D-2D Coupling & Exchange Visualization**
*   **Purpose:** To illustrate how parameters control the interaction between the pipe network and the surface model.
*   **Interactive Diagram:**
    *   A side-view cross-section of a manhole with a ground surface above.
    *   **Key Parameters as Inputs:**`Exchange Time Step`,`Weir Coefficient`,`Orifice Coefficient`.
    *   **Animation:** Water rises in the manhole above the crown.
        *   Flow begins to exit to the 2D surface mesh as an orifice flow (visualized as jets).
        *   As the surface floods, water can flow back into the manhole as weir flow (visualized as over-the-rim flow).
    *   **Interaction:** Adjusting the`Exchange Time Step` makes the data transfer between 1D and 2D models happen in faster/slower "chunks." Changing coefficients alters the flow rate arrows.
    *   **Educational Goal:** Makes the complex coupling process concrete, showing where key parameters apply.
